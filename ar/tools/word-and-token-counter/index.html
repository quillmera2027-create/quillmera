عن أداة عدّاد الكلمات والرموز

عدّاد كلمات مجاني: احسب الكلمات والأحرف وقدّر عدد الرموز (Tokens) أثناء الكتابة أو اللصق. فوري وخاص ولا يُرفع نصك ولا يلزم تسجيل. تعمل الأداة داخل متصفحك، فيبقى ما تكتبه على جهازك وتظهر النتيجة فورًا.

طريقة استعمال عدّاد الكلمات والرموز

  1. اكتب المدخلات أو الصقها في الصندوق أعلاه.
  2. اختر الخيار الذي تحتاجه إن عرضت الأداة خيارات.
  3. انسخ النتيجة أو حمّلها كملف.

How to write a product description that sells

A good description answers one question: why should this person buy this item today? Give the AI the material, the size or fit, who it is for, and what problem it solves. Without those facts it fills the gap with vague praise.

Ask for benefits before features. "Soft cotton" is a feature; "stays comfortable through a long workday" is a benefit. Request two versions of different lengths, use the short one for listings and the long one for the product page, and remove any claim you cannot prove.

A cold email template that gets replies

Cold emails work when they are short, specific and easy to answer. Tell the AI who the reader is, why you picked them, and the one small thing you want, such as a 15-minute call.

  • Open with a detail about their business, not about you.
  • State your offer in one sentence.
  • End with a single question that can be answered with yes or no.

Keep it under 120 words and ask the AI for three subject lines you can test.

Five rules for better ChatGPT prompts

  • Say who you are and who the answer is for.
  • Describe the task in one clear sentence.
  • Give the facts the AI cannot guess.
  • Set the tone, length and format you want.
  • Ask it to check with you if something is missing.

These five steps remove most disappointing answers. The AI prompt generator in the tools section follows them automatically, so you can start from its output and adjust the details.

Customer support replies that calm an upset customer

An unhappy customer wants three things: to feel heard, to know what happens next, and to get it fixed. Paste the customer's message into your prompt and ask the AI to acknowledge the problem in the first sentence, offer a clear next step, and avoid defensive wording.

Tell it your real policy, for example refund within 14 days, so it does not promise things you cannot do. Read the reply once before sending and add the customer's name and order number.

How to plan a week of social media posts with AI

Give the AI your business, your audience and two or three goals, such as more sign-ups or more repeat buyers. Ask for seven posts with a mix of formats: a tip, a customer story, a behind-the-scenes post, a product highlight, a question, a short promotion and a recap.

Request a hook line, the post text and a suggested image for each. Then rewrite the hooks in your own voice. Posts that sound like you outperform posts that sound generic.

How to summarize a long document with AI

State the purpose first: a summary for a busy manager is different from one for a student. Ask for a fixed structure, for instance five key points, any deadlines or numbers, and open questions.

For very long files, summarize section by section and then ask for a summary of the summaries. Always check names, dates and figures against the original, because summaries can drop or blur details.

Writing a project proposal clients approve

Clients approve proposals that show they understand the problem. Give the AI the client's goal, your approach, the timeline and the price, and ask for these sections: the problem, the solution, the deliverables, the schedule and the next step.

Keep it to two pages. Ask the AI to remove jargon and to explain each deliverable in terms of what the client gets, not what you do.

Role prompting: why "act as" works

When you ask the AI to act as an experienced copywriter or support agent, you narrow the style and vocabulary it uses. The answer becomes more focused than a reply to a generic request.

Choose a role that matches the job and add the audience, for example "a copywriter writing for busy parents." A role does not add knowledge the AI lacks, so you still need to supply the facts.

Common AI prompt mistakes

  • Being too vague, such as "write something about my shop."
  • Skipping the audience, so the tone is wrong.
  • Packing five tasks into one request.
  • Accepting the first draft without asking for changes.

Fix them by splitting big jobs into steps and by replying with specific edits like "shorter, more direct, remove the second paragraph."

How to check AI output before you publish it

AI can state wrong facts with confidence. Before publishing, verify every number, name, date and quote. Remove claims about results, health or law that you cannot support.

Read the text aloud to catch generic phrasing, and add something only you know: a real example, a customer detail or a price. That personal touch is what makes content useful and trustworthy.

How to build a monthly budget that works

Start with your take-home pay, then list fixed costs: rent, utilities, transport and debt payments. A simple rule is 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt.

Track your spending for one month, compare it with your plan and trim one category at a time. The Family budget 50/30/20 tool does the maths for you.

How to spot phishing emails and scams

Scammers rely on urgency: a blocked account, a missed delivery, a prize. Check the sender address, hover over links before clicking and be wary of unexpected attachments.

When in doubt, do not reply. Type the official website address yourself, never share passwords or codes, then report and delete the message.

Strong passwords and two-factor authentication

Long beats complex: use a passphrase of four or more random words and a different password for every account. A password manager remembers them for you.

Turn on two-factor authentication for email, banking and social accounts, ideally with an authenticator app. Try our Password generator.

Simple habits for better sleep

Keep a regular schedule, even on weekends. Most adults need seven to nine hours. Keep the bedroom dark, cool and quiet, and avoid caffeine in the afternoon.

Put screens away an hour before bed. Our Sleep calculator suggests bedtimes based on 90-minute cycles. If problems persist, talk to a doctor.

How to build an emergency fund

An emergency fund covers surprises like a car repair or job loss. Aim first for a small cushion, then build towards three to six months of essential expenses.

Automate a transfer on payday, keep it in a separate easy-access account and use it only for real emergencies. The Savings goal tool shows how long it will take.

How to write a CV that gets interviews

Keep it to one or two pages with your most relevant experience first. Use clear headings and a simple layout that screening software can read.

Describe results, not duties: "cut response time by 30%" beats "handled support". Tailor the top third to each job ad and proofread it twice.

How to prepare for a job interview

Research the company, re-read the job ad and prepare three examples of your work using the situation, action, result format.

Practice answers aloud, prepare two questions to ask, test your route or video link early and send a short thank-you note afterwards.

Eating well on a budget

Plan meals for the week, shop with a list and compare unit prices. Frozen vegetables, beans, lentils, eggs and oats are cheap and nutritious.

Cook larger batches, freeze portions and use leftovers. Our Weekly meal planner and Recipe scaler help you organise it.

How to protect your privacy online

Review app permissions, keep software updated and use different email addresses for different services. Check what your social profiles show publicly.

Be careful on public Wi-Fi, avoid sharing sensitive data in chats and read the privacy settings of new apps. Only the data you share can be misused.

Study smarter, not longer

Short focused sessions of 25 to 50 minutes with breaks beat long marathons. Remove distractions and set one clear goal per session.

Test yourself instead of rereading, and review after one day, one week and one month. Explaining a topic in your own words shows what you still miss.
